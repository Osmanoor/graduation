**"GaQR: An Efficient Generation-augmented Question Rewriter"**

### **Citation**
> Young, O., Fan, Y., Zhang, R., Guo, J., de Rijke, M., & Cheng, X. (2024). **GaQR: An Efficient Generation-augmented Question Rewriter**. In *Proceedings of the 33rd ACM International Conference on Information and Knowledge Management (CIKM ’24)* (pp. 4228–4232). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/3627673.3679930

---

### **1. Methodology**
The authors propose **GaQR**, a pipeline designed to improve search recall by rewriting queries using Large Language Models (LLMs) while mitigating two common LLM issues: **hallucination** (generating false information) and **high latency** (slow response times).

The methodology consists of three distinct phases:

#### **Phase 1: Generation-Augmented Rewriter (The Teacher)**
Instead of simply asking the LLM to rewrite a query using synonyms, GaQR uses a **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)** approach to think "one step ahead."
*   **Prompting Strategy:** It uses a two-step prompt.
    1.  **Pseudo-Answer Generation:** The model is first asked to generate a hypothetical answer to the query.
    2.  **Query Generation:** The model uses the original query *and* the generated pseudo-answer to formulate new, more detailed search queries.
*   **Teacher Model:** They utilize **GPT-3.5-instruct**.

#### **Phase 2: Relevance Verification Retriever (The Filter)**
To solve the hallucination problem, the system filters out "bad" rewrites generated by the teacher.
*   **Process:** The system performs retrieval using the **Original Query** and the **Rewritten Queries**.
*   **Metric:** It compares the **Recall** of the documents retrieved.
*   **Selection:** If a rewritten query achieves higher recall than the original, it is kept as a valid training example. If it performs worse (indicating the LLM likely hallucinated or drifted off-topic), it is discarded.
*   **Retriever Used:** They primarily use **BM25** as the verification retriever to filter the data.

#### **Phase 3: Knowledge Distillation-based Rewriter (The Student)**
To solve the latency problem, the authors distill the knowledge from the large Teacher model into a smaller Student model.
*   **Training Data:** The verified/filtered queries from Phase 2 are used as the dataset ($D$).
*   **Student Model:** They fine-tune **Llama2-7b-chat** using this dataset.
*   **Objective:** The student learns to generate the high-quality rewrites directly from the input query without needing the computationally expensive two-step CoT process during inference.

### **2. Experimental Setup**
The experiments were designed to test the **generalization ability** of the student model (training on one set of datasets and testing on completely different domains).

*   **Training Datasets:**
    *   **MS MARCO** (Passage Ranking)
    *   **MIRACL** (Multilingual retrieval, though likely used the English subset here).
    *   *Note:* They selected a subset of 6,000 queries total from these to fine-tune the model.
*   **Evaluation Datasets (Out-of-Domain):**
    *   They tested on 5 datasets from the **BEIR benchmark**: SciFact, NFCorpus, ArguAna, FiQA-2018, and TREC-COVID.
*   **Evaluation Metric:** **Recall@1000 (R@1k)** (Since the goal is to improve the initial retrieval stage).
*   **Training Hyperparameters:**
    *   Learning rate: 2e-5
    *   Epochs: 5
    *   Temperature: 0.2

### **3. Baselines**
The paper compares GaQR against several strong baselines, categorized by the retrieval method used.

#### **A. Lexical Retrieval Baselines**
*   **BM25:** The primary baseline.
    *   **Implementation:** They used the **Anserini** toolkit implementation.
    *   **Parameters:** Default settings ($k_1 = 0.9$, $b = 0.4$).
*   **QL (Query Likelihood):** Dirichlet smoothing implementation from Anserini.

#### **B. Dense Retrieval Baselines**
The authors tested how well GaQR improves performance when paired with dense retrievers (embedding-based search).
*   **TAS-B:** (Topic-Aware Sampling BERT).
*   **ANCE:** (Approximate Nearest Neighbor Negative Contrastive Estimation).
*   **DPR:** (Dense Passage Retriever).
*   *Implementation:* These models were sourced from the **BEIR benchmark** repository.

#### **C. Sparse / Expansion Baselines**
*   **DocT5Query:** A document expansion method that generates queries for documents offline (the inverse of GaQR).
*   **SPARTA:** A sparse transformer matching retrieval method.

#### **D. LLM-based Baselines**
*   **Query2Doc (Q2D):** Generates a pseudo-document and appends it to the query.
*   **CoT:** Standard Chain-of-Thought prompting without the distillation/filtering pipeline.

---

### **Key Results**
1.  **Performance:** GaQR improved retrieval performance by **3% to 15%** on datasets like MIRACL and NFCorpus compared to BM25.
2.  **Generalization:** Even though it was trained on MS MARCO/MIRACL, it improved performance on medical (NFCorpus) and financial (FiQA) datasets.
3.  **Efficiency:** The distilled student model was **4x to 9x faster** than standard Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods and comparable in speed to simpler expansion methods like Q2E, making it viable for real-time search.
4.  **Baseline Discrepancy (Critical Note):** The paper reports a BM25 Recall@100 of **70.71** for MIRACL, which is significantly lower than the standard result of **~88.2** reported in the original MIRACL paper. This indicates the authors utilized a weak baseline (likely due to applying MS MARCO-specific parameters $k=0.9, b=0.4$ to the MIRACL dataset), which may inflate the reported relative gains for that specific dataset.
