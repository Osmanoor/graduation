- **Short Description:** This paper introduces Omni-RAG, a framework designed to enhance the robustness and effectiveness of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems in live, open-domain environments. By leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for query denoising and intent decomposition, the system aims to generate accurate responses to noisy, ambiguous, and multi-intent user queries.

- **Research Question:** How can RAG systems be optimized to robustly interpret and answer complex, noisy, and multi-intent queries in real-time applications, where inputs differ significantly from the clean data typically used in standard benchmarks?

- **Main Methodology:** The authors propose the Omni-RAG pipeline, which consists of three key modules:
    1.  **Deep Query Understanding and Decomposition:** An LLM preprocesses user input to correct noise (e.g., spelling errors) and decomposes complex queries into structured sub-queries.
    2.  **Intent-Aware Knowledge Retrieval:** The system performs retrieval for each specific sub-query using OpenSearch over the corpus, aggregating the results to ensure broad coverage.
    3.  **Reranking and Generation:** A BGE reranker selects the top-10 most relevant documents from the aggregated results, which are then fed into a Falcon-10B model using Chain-of-Thought prompting to generate the final response.

- **Dataset & Benchmark:**
    -   **Competition:** SIGIR 2025 LiveRAG Challenge (Session 1).
    -   **Knowledge Corpus:** FineWeb dataset.
    -   **Query Source:** Synthetic queries generated by the DataMorgana simulator to mimic live human interactions.
    -   **Evaluation Metrics:** "Correctness" (Relevance) and "Faithfulness," evaluated using pseudo-labels generated by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Falcon-10B.

- **Research Contributions:**
    The primary contribution of this work is the development of Omni-RAG, a pipeline that effectively bridges the gap between theoretical RAG capabilities and the demands of real-world, live applications. By prioritizing query understanding—specifically through rewriting and decomposing inputs into sub-intents—the framework successfully addresses the semantic noise and ambiguity that cause standard RAG systems to fail. This intent-aware approach ensures that retrieval mechanisms target specific information needs rather than broad, noisy signals.

    Empirically, the framework demonstrated significant effectiveness, achieving Rank-2 overall in Session 1 of the SIGIR 2025 LiveRAG Challenge. The study provides valuable insights into system optimization, revealing that while performance scales well with increased document counts, simply increasing reasoning paths (self-consistency) offers diminishing returns on faithfulness. Additionally, the paper establishes a practical methodology for using pseudo-labeling to evaluate RAG systems in open-domain settings lacking ground-truth answers.
