# Dedication & Acknowledgements — Elicitation Questions

**How to use this:** Mohammed and Osman answer separately, in writing, in whatever
language comes first (Arabic is fine — I'll translate). Don't polish. Fragments are
better than sentences. Skip any question that's blank for you.

**Why moments, not categories:** "family, friends, country" produces generic
sentences. A specific afternoon in a specific room produces a real one. Every
question below asks for a moment or a specific person, on purpose.

---

## Part 1 — Dedication (emotional, to people, no thanking)

**D1.** Think of one specific moment in the last 8 years when you nearly gave up on
this degree. Where were you? What was happening? Who or what got you past it?

**D2.** Who paid a price for this degree that they never once complained about?
What did they give up? (Money, a room in the house, their own plans, sleep, safety.)

**D3.** Is there someone who should be reading this thesis but won't be? Who? Why not
— died, left the country, lost touch?

**D4.** Name one thing the war took that was specifically yours. A building, a room,
a routine, a walk to campus, a shop, a person. Be concrete — the name of the place.

**D5.** Who believed you'd finish this before you believed it yourself? What did they
actually say to you? Try to remember the words.

**D6.** Your batch — the people you started with in first year. One shared memory
that sums up all 8 years. Funny is fine. Funny is often better.

**D7.** When this thesis is bound and printed, who do you want to hand it to first,
and what do you want them to feel when they open it?

**D8.** Is there anyone you're *not* dedicating this to, that people might expect?
(Worth knowing so the wording doesn't accidentally include them.)

## Part 2 — Acknowledgements (specific, about the work)

**A1.** Dr. Tahani — one specific thing she said or did that changed the direction of
this work. A comment on a draft, a question in a meeting, something she refused to
let you get away with.

**A2.** Which other teacher in the department taught you something that actually
shows up in this thesis? Which course, which idea?

**A3.** Who helped you technically outside your supervisor? A friend who debugged
something at 2am, someone who explained BM25, someone on a forum or Discord?

**A4.** Who gave you *resources*, not advice? A laptop, internet access, money for
API credits, a quiet room, electricity, a generator, a place to stay?

**A5.** Who read your drafts and told you the truth about them?

**A6.** Which staff kept the department running when it would have been easier to
stop? Administrators, lab technicians, anyone whose name doesn't appear on a
lecture timetable.

**A7.** Family — not "their support". One concrete thing. A meal at a strange hour,
a phone bill paid, a question they stopped asking because they knew it stressed you.

**A8.** Did anything or anyone outside the university make this possible — an
employer who gave you time off, a scholarship, an institution that hosted you after
displacement?

## Part 3 — Memory joggers (2018–2026)

You said you can't remember what mattered. Read these and see what surfaces. Don't
answer them directly — just note anything that comes back.

- **Dec 2018 – Apr 2019:** the revolution. U of K students were at the centre of it.
  Where were you? What did that year do to your studies?
- **2019–2020:** COVID. Campus closed. How did you study?
- **April 2023:** the war starts. Where were you on day one? Where did you go?
- **After:** displacement, remote study, the department trying to teach anyway.
- **This project:** the first experiment that worked. The first one that failed
  badly. The night before a deadline. The Colab session that died mid-run.
- **Right now:** what does finishing actually mean to you, after all of that?

## Part 4 — Mechanics (quick answers, 30 seconds each)

**M1.** Joint dedication (one voice, "we") or two separate dedications, one each?
**M2.** Do you want anyone named by full name, or kept as "my mother", "our friends"?
**M3.** Exact spelling of every name you want printed — including Dr. Tahani's full
title as she prefers it.
**M4.** Any Qur'anic verse, line of poetry, or proverb either of you wants to open
with? (Common in Sudanese theses. Optional.)
**M5.** How long? Current draft is ~1 page dedication, ~1 page acknowledgements.
Longer is fine — this is the one section nobody marks you down for.
