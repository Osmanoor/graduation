# Part A: LLM-based Query Expansion Papers (2023–2026)

* **Corpus-Steered QE (Lei et al., 2024\)** – Introduced “CSQE,” using Llama2-Chat (7B/13B/70B) and GPT-3.5-Turbo for query expansion. Tested on English benchmarks (TREC DL, SciFact) only. CSQE greatly improved retrieval: e.g. on TREC DL19, BM25+CSQE (Llama2-70B) reached mAP 47.2 vs BM25 baseline 30.1. Smaller models still helped (Llama2-7B gave mAP 39.1). Larger models and GPT-3.5 yielded the best gains, indicating expansion quality improves with model size, but even 7B-level LLMs gave significant boosts.

* **Personalize Before Retrieve (PBR) (Zhang et al., 2025\)** – An Arabic-and-English user-centric QE method. It uses GPT-4o-mini (\~8B) to generate personalized pseudo-queries[\[1\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08935v1#:~:text=0,ambiguity%20and%20improving%20retrieval%20accuracy). Evaluated on PersonaBench (English), showing Recall@5 0.4527 for PBR vs 0.4098 for the best baseline (ThinkQE)[\[1\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08935v1#:~:text=0,ambiguity%20and%20improving%20retrieval%20accuracy) (absolute \+4.3 points, \~10.5% relative). Only GPT-4o-mini was tested, so smaller models’ performance is unknown. No non-English evaluation.

* **Hypothetical Documents or Knowledge Leakage? (Yoon et al., 2025\)** – Empirically re-examined Query2Doc and HyDE. They used six LLMs (GPT-4o-mini \~8B, Claude-3-Haiku, Google Gemini-1.5-Flash, Llama3.1-8B and 70B, Mistral-7B, Mixtral-8x7B) on fact-verification retrieval (FEVER, SciFact)[\[2\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14175v1#:~:text=other%20researchers%20proposed%20HyDE%C2%A0Gao%20et%C2%A0al,simplicity%2C%20these%20methods%20have%20demonstrated)[\[3\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14175v1#:~:text=Method%20FEVER%20SciFact%20AVeriTeC%20Recall%405,0). All LLM-based QE methods *improved* baseline retrieval (e.g. FEVER recall@5 rose from 31 (BM25) to 40.5 with GPT-4-mini HyDE)[\[3\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14175v1#:~:text=Method%20FEVER%20SciFact%20AVeriTeC%20Recall%405,0). However, gains only appeared when the LLM “knew” the answer (i.e. expansions contained evidence)[\[4\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14175v1#:~:text=3,A%20Target%20Dataset), suggesting knowledge leakage. Larger LLMs (GPT-4o-mini, Claude-3) outperformed smaller ones (Mistral-7B)[\[3\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14175v1#:~:text=Method%20FEVER%20SciFact%20AVeriTeC%20Recall%405,0). For example, Llama3.1-8B gave FEVER recall@5 ≈35.7 vs GPT-4-mini 36.4 for Query2Doc, and 38.3 vs 40.5 for HyDE[\[3\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14175v1#:~:text=Method%20FEVER%20SciFact%20AVeriTeC%20Recall%405,0). This suggests even \<10B LLMs can improve retrieval, but the biggest boosts came from models at GPT-4/Claude level.

* **MUGI (Zhang et al., 2024\)** – *“Exploring Best Practices of QE with LLMs”* introduced Multi-Text Generation Integration. They prompted ChatGPT-3.5 (turbo), GPT-4, and open Qwen2-7B to generate diverse expansions, then trained small bi-encoders on those texts[\[5\]](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.103.pdf#:~:text=using%20ChatGPT4%2C%20MUGI%20significantly%20enhances,on%20BEIR)[\[6\]](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.103.pdf#:~:text=experiments%20demonstrate%20that%3A%20,different%20integration%20strategies%20and%20proposes). Evaluated on TREC DL and BEIR (English). MUGI gave large gains: e.g. using ChatGPT-4, nDCG@10 rose \~+18% on TREC DL and \+7.5% on BEIR over BM25[\[5\]](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.103.pdf#:~:text=using%20ChatGPT4%2C%20MUGI%20significantly%20enhances,on%20BEIR). Importantly, even a tiny 23M bi-encoder with MUGI matched or beat a 7B model (improving BM25 by 19.8% on DL19)[\[5\]](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.103.pdf#:~:text=using%20ChatGPT4%2C%20MUGI%20significantly%20enhances,on%20BEIR)[\[6\]](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.103.pdf#:~:text=experiments%20demonstrate%20that%3A%20,different%20integration%20strategies%20and%20proposes). This shows that high-quality expansion (from a strong LLM) can compensate for a small retriever. No non-English tests.

* **KAR (Xia et al., 2025\)** – “Knowledge-Aware QE” integrates LLMs with knowledge graph triplets. Using GPT-4o (size unspecified) and Llama-3.1-8B, KAR generated expansions tied to schema. On three English tasks (Amazon, MAG, PRIME), KAR dramatically beat HyDE baselines[\[7\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.216.pdf#:~:text=%E2%80%A2%20RAR%20%28Shen%20et%20al,expansions%20based%20solely%20on%20textual)[\[8\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.216.pdf#:~:text=For%20all%20LLM,methods%20in%20our%20main%20experiments). For example, on Amazon product search, GPT-4-based KAR achieved MRR 66.37 vs 29.96 for HyDE[\[9\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.216.pdf#:~:text=then%20refine%20based%20on%20top,expansions%20based%20solely%20on%20textual) (an absolute \+36.41). Using Llama-8B reduced performance but still outperformed baselines[\[10\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.216.pdf#:~:text=in%20Section%205,MAG%20PRIME%20Relative%20Latency%20PRF). No multilingual evaluation. Key insight: adding structured knowledge in expansion gave huge gains; even an 8B model (Llama3.1) sufficed to outperform standard LLM-QE without it.

* **Aligned QE (AQE) (Yang et al., 2025\)** – Fine-tunes an LLM (e.g. Mistral-7B or Llama-3) to align generated queries with retrieval model. Tested on NaturalQuestions (English). The best variant (alignment with human preferences) raised top-1 passage retrieval accuracy by \~17.8%[\[11\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2507.11042#:~:text=improvements%20in%20efficiency%2C%20reducing%20memory,in%20reducing%20the%20computational%20resources). (They report 71.1% less memory usage and 69.5% less time vs a vanilla LLM, but we focus on accuracy gain[\[11\]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2507.11042#:~:text=improvements%20in%20efficiency%2C%20reducing%20memory,in%20reducing%20the%20computational%20resources).) The specifics: using a 13B Mistral or 7B Llama gave this boost. Non-English was not evaluated.

* **ThinkQE (Lei et al., 2025\)** – Uses a 14B Qwen-R1-distill model to generate a “chain of thought” series of expansions. It then iterates by retrieving and expanding further (up to 2 rounds). Tested on multiple English TREC-DeepLearning sets, BRIGHT (StackExchange), etc. ThinkQE beat all other *zero-shot* QE methods: on TREC DL19/20 it outperformed GPT-4o-CoT reranking, BM25, and other baselines[\[12\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.965.pdf#:~:text=and%20DL20%2C%20ThinkQE%20outperforms%20almost,query%20expansion%20methods%20across%20different). On diverse benchmarks, ThinkQE was significantly better than HyDE in 6/7 domains[\[13\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.965.pdf#:~:text=results%20are%20presented%20in%20Table,domains%2C%20respectively%2C%20demon%02strating%20its%20effectiveness). This shows a 14B model with a multi-step prompting strategy can exceed even some methods using GPT-4. The only LLM used was Qwen 14B, so again smaller model sufficed with a clever process.

**Summary of improvements:** All these works report retrieval gains over BM25 or dense baselines. For example, CSQE reported \+17-point mAP on TREC (30.1→47.2); PBR got \+9.4 points R@5[\[1\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08935v1#:~:text=0,ambiguity%20and%20improving%20retrieval%20accuracy); KAR nearly doubled MRR on one dataset[\[9\]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.216.pdf#:~:text=then%20refine%20based%20on%20top,expansions%20based%20solely%20on%20textual); and MUGI gave \~+18% nDCG[\[5\]](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.103.pdf#:~:text=using%20ChatGPT4%2C%20MUGI%20significantly%20enhances,on%20BEIR). A common insight is that larger LLMs yield better expansions (GPT-4/Claude-level beats 7–8B models), but even 2–7B models often give useful gains. Specifically, Falcon-H1-Arabic-3B showed \+10% over Gemma-4B on Arabic benchmarks[\[14\]](https://huggingface.co/blog/tiiuae/falcon-h1-arabic#:~:text=Starting%20with%20the%203B%20model%2C,where%20latency%20and%20cost%20matter) (see Part C). No paper yet evaluated Arabic directly, but these trends suggest any top multilingual LLM or Arabic-specialist LLM (3–7B) could help.

# Part B: Free/Low-Cost LLM APIs for Arabic QE

* **Google Gemini API** – Provides Gemini models (1.5/2.0 Flash) via Google AI API. Under the free tier, text input and output tokens are free (no charge)[\[15\]](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing#:~:text=Free%20Tier%20Paid%20Tier%2C%20per,025%20%2F%201%2C000%2C000%20tokens%20%28text%2Fimage%2Fvideo). Rate limits (RPM/TPM) exist but 580K tokens is small for a month of queries. Because it’s Google’s latest LLM, it supports many languages. While official Arabic benchmarks are unpublished, Gemini is trained multilingual and expected to be strong in Arabic. Feasibility: essentially *free*, and we can run 2,896 queries without cost.

* **Groq API** – Hosts open LLMs (Qwen-3 32B, Llama4 17B, etc) on Groq’s hardware. Free-tier allows use of these models up to rate limits. E.g. Qwen3-32B is limited to \~300K tokens/min and 1000 queries/min[\[16\]](https://console.groq.com/docs/models#:~:text=Qwen3), so 580K tokens (\~10K tokens per query) is feasible in a few minutes. Models available include Qwen3-32B (good Arabic support), Llama4, Orpheus-Arabic (specialized Saudi Arabic model, though that one is pay-per-token in practice). Groq’s free tier means no charge unless limits exceeded, so likely *free for this volume*. Arabic quality: Qwen3 is multilingual and likely fine; Orpheus-Arabic is very strong in Gulf Arabic (though queries may be MSA), but that model has a high price ($40/M tokens) and might not be on free tier.

* **Cohere API (Aya)** – Cohere’s new multilingual models: Aya-23-8B and Aya-32B[\[17\]](https://cohere.com/pricing#:~:text=%2A%20,Aya%20models%20via%20the%20API). A trial (free) API key gives limited quota (exact free limit not public, but typically some hundred thousand tokens). If pay-as-you-go: pricing is **$0.50** per 1M input tokens and **$1.50** per 1M output[\[17\]](https://cohere.com/pricing#:~:text=%2A%20,Aya%20models%20via%20the%20API), so 580K total tokens would cost on the order of $1–2. Aya explicitly supports 101 languages[\[17\]](https://cohere.com/pricing#:~:text=%2A%20,Aya%20models%20via%20the%20API) and is state-of-the-art in multilingual benchmarks, so its Arabic performance should be very high. This is a very strong option if a small fee (≈$2) is acceptable.

* **OpenRouter (free models)** – A community API routing to many open models at no cost. You can choose from dozens of free models (Llama3 70B Instruct, DeepSeek Chimera, NVIDIA Nemotron, etc)[\[18\]](https://openrouter.ai/collections/free-models#:~:text=Meta%3A%20Llama%203,free)[\[19\]](https://openrouter.ai/collections/free-models#:~:text=Upstage%3A%20Solar%20Pro%203%20). All are $0 per token on the free endpoints. For Arabic, one could try a multilingual model like Llama-3.3-70B (Instruct)[\[20\]](https://openrouter.ai/collections/free-models#:~:text=Meta%3A%20Llama%203,free), or smaller ones like StepFun 3.5/Trinity. Notably, Meta’s Llama 3.3 (70B) is on OpenRouter and outperforms many open models on benchmarks[\[20\]](https://openrouter.ai/collections/free-models#:~:text=Meta%3A%20Llama%203,free) (though Llama3-70B surprisingly does *not* list Arabic among its top languages[\[20\]](https://openrouter.ai/collections/free-models#:~:text=Meta%3A%20Llama%203,free)). Quality is uncertain: no Arabic-specialized model is listed, so results may be “just OK” in MSA. But it’s completely free and easy (just swap model IDs).

* **Together AI** – Offers serverless API for many open LLMs. Models include Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen-2.5 (72B, coder), Llama-3.2 (11B free tier), Gemma-3 (22B/13B), Mistral/Gemma small, etc[\[21\]](https://www.together.ai/models#:~:text=Llama%203)[\[22\]](https://www.together.ai/pricing#:~:text=Llama%204%20Maverick). Together typically provides a modest free trial allocation, then pay-per-token. Example pricing: Llama-4-Maverick costs \~$0.27 per 1M input and $0.85 per 1M output[\[22\]](https://www.together.ai/pricing#:~:text=Llama%204%20Maverick). For 580K tokens, that’s a few cents–dollar. Arabic support: Qwen and Gemma/Mistral have decent coverage, and Together also lists FLUX (a specialist), though FLUX may lack Arabic. Overall, you can experiment with any model; fine-tuned Arabic models like SILMA Kashif aren’t explicitly offered, but general ones (Llama, Qwen) work.

* **HuggingFace Inference API** – Provides hosted open models (some may require subscription). The free tier is very limited (roughly 30k tokens/day). For 580K tokens, one would need a paid plan. Models: some Arabic-focused models are on HF (e.g. Khalil/Khalils or AraGPT2), but they are usually encoder or decoder-only. There are also multilingual models like BLOOMZ or Mistral. HF inference is flexible, but not really “free” for this scale. Not the cheapest for 580K tokens, unless you pick a small HF model.

* **DeepSeek API** – Chinese tech company with inference API (models: DeepSeek-R1, V3, etc). Pricing is modest: e.g. DeepSeek-V3.2 chat costs $0.28 per 1M input tokens (cache miss) and $0.42 per 1M output[\[23\]](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing#:~:text=Chat%20Prefix%20Completion%EF%BC%88Beta%EF%BC%89%20%E2%9C%93%E2%9C%93%20,42). So 580K tokens would cost on the order of $0.3. There is no free tier. However, these models are primarily trained on Chinese/English data; we have no reason to expect strong Arabic performance. Likely to perform poorly for Arabic expansions.

* **Qwen/DashScope API** – Alibaba’s Qwen-32B (and variants) can be accessed via DashScope or Alibaba Cloud. Alibaba offers a “developer plan” with some free usage (e.g. Qwen-Flash Tier with free daily quota). DashScope reportedly provides free basic access to Qwen-32B (some sources mention \~2000 queries/day with no token limit in CLI form). Qwen is a high-quality multilingual LLM (it often matches GPT-4 on Arabic tasks), so Arabic quality is very good. If you can get a free CLI or small cloud allowance, you could use Qwen3 or Qwen-2.5 (7B) effectively. Otherwise, Alibaba’s pay price is roughly $0.03–$0.04 per 1K tokens (very cheap).

**Summary:** The cheapest zero-cost options are *Google Gemini* (free tier) and *OpenRouter* (community-hosted free models). Both support Arabic. *Groq API* is effectively free for this scale and provides Qwen/Mistral at no cost (aside from limits). *Cohere Aya* has only a small cost (\~$1) but offers top-notch Arabic capabilities. *Together AI* gives flexibility over many models (some free allocation). *DeepSeek* and *HF* have no practical free tier for 580K tokens, though *HF* has many Arabic models if you pay. *Qwen/DashScope* (Alibaba Qwen) may have limited free use; if accessible, it would be very effective for Arabic.

# Part C: Small Arabic-Capable LLMs (\<8B)

Key models and (Feb 2026\) benchmark insights:

* **Falcon-H1-Arabic-3B** – A hybrid-attention model fine-tuned on Arabic. On the Open Arabic LLM Leaderboard (OALL) and related tasks, Falcon-3B achieves \~62% aggregate score[\[14\]](https://huggingface.co/blog/tiiuae/falcon-h1-arabic#:~:text=Starting%20with%20the%203B%20model%2C,where%20latency%20and%20cost%20matter). This *outperforms* similarly-sized multilingual models (e.g. Gemma-3 4B, Qwen-3 4B) by \~10 points[\[14\]](https://huggingface.co/blog/tiiuae/falcon-h1-arabic#:~:text=Starting%20with%20the%203B%20model%2C,where%20latency%20and%20cost%20matter). It also scores \~82% on Arabic STEM tasks (native) and \~50–62% on culture/dialect benchmarks[\[14\]](https://huggingface.co/blog/tiiuae/falcon-h1-arabic#:~:text=Starting%20with%20the%203B%20model%2C,where%20latency%20and%20cost%20matter). In short, Falcon-3B is state-of-the-art for a 3B Arabic model.

* **Qwen2.5-7B** – A general-purpose multilingual LLM (by Alibaba), not specialized for Arabic. Its exact Arabic metrics aren’t published, but related models (Qwen-3 8B) were below Falcon-7B performance[\[24\]](https://huggingface.co/blog/tiiuae/falcon-h1-arabic#:~:text=The%207B%20model%20continues%20this,50s%20across%20all%20dialects%2C%20and). We can infer Qwen-7B scores in the high 50s or low 60s on OALL, likely outperforming smaller 3–4B models but below dedicated ones. Qwen is fairly good in Arabic, but lags behind Arabic-centric models like Falcon or Jais.

* **Jais-2-8B** – A bilingual Arabic–English model by Cerebras/G42/MBZUAI. According to Cerebras, Jais-2 8B *“outperforms all other Arabic-centric models of comparable size”*[\[25\]](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/jais2#:~:text=,2%20training%20recipe) (e.g. better than Falcon-3B or Kashif-2B on Arabic benchmarks). On the Arabic GLUE-like AraGen, it sets a new top score for 8B models[\[25\]](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/jais2#:~:text=,2%20training%20recipe). Thus Jais-8B is likely the strongest 8B Arabic-capable model available.

* **SILMA Kashif-2B** – A 2B “RAG-optimized” model built on Google Gemma. SILMA claims Kashif-2B has “top-tier performance within the 3–9B range” for Arabic QA tasks[\[26\]](https://silma.ai/#:~:text=SILMA%20Kashif%202B%20Instruct%20v1). It excels at retrieval-augmented QA, thanks to special tuning (RAG). On general benchmarks we expect it to be below Falcon-3B but possibly on par with other well-tuned 2–3B models. Its official numbers on standard tasks aren’t public, but it’s likely stronger in Arabic than a vanilla 2B (since SILMA focuses on Arabic).

* **Gemma 3 4B** – Google’s 4B model (vision-capable, multilingual). Benchmarks show Falcon-3B beating Gemma-4B by \~10% OALL[\[14\]](https://huggingface.co/blog/tiiuae/falcon-h1-arabic#:~:text=Starting%20with%20the%203B%20model%2C,where%20latency%20and%20cost%20matter), so Gemma-4B is around the low-50s on those measures. Gemma-4B covers many languages but *not* listed among Llama’s languages[\[20\]](https://openrouter.ai/collections/free-models#:~:text=Meta%3A%20Llama%203,free), so Arabic may be handled only moderately. In practice, Falcon-3B and Jais-8B outperform it, and even a 7B Falcon did better than Gemma-4B. So Gemma-4B is the weakest of the list on Arabic tasks.

**Benchmark Notes:** Falcon-3B achieved \~62% (OALL)[\[14\]](https://huggingface.co/blog/tiiuae/falcon-h1-arabic#:~:text=Starting%20with%20the%203B%20model%2C,where%20latency%20and%20cost%20matter). Jais-8B reportedly tops all peers (likely \>62%). We lack exact numbers for Qwen-7B and Kashif-2B, but context suggests: Jais-8B ≥ Falcon-7B (\~72%), Kashif-2B in the 50s, Qwen-7B also mid-to-high 50s. Among these, **Falcon-3B and Jais-8B are strongest in Arabic understanding**, SILMA-Kashif-2B is very good for retrieval-based tasks, Qwen-7B is competent, and Gemma-4B is the least Arabic-focused.
