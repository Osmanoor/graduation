## Part 1: Direct Answers to Your 10 Questions

### 1. Chapter Summary Sections
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** They are **optional, but recommended to keep**.
* **Details:** You are not strictly required to have a "Chapter Summary" at the end of each chapter, but keeping them is **excellent** ("ممتاز"). It provides a clean recap of the chapter’s contents and does not disrupt the flow of the thesis.

### 2. Scope of Problem Statement (General vs. Specific)
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** It must be **very specific**, using a funnel approach (*"from wide to narrow"*).
* **Details:** You should start from the broader area (Arabic Information Retrieval/RAG challenges) and narrow down progressively until you arrive at the **exact, specific problem** your thesis tackles (e.g., short-query information poverty, vocabulary mismatch, and model scale limitations). 
* **Format:** While bullet points are acceptable, Dr. Tahani **strongly prefers a single, well-structured, comprehensive paragraph** (even if long) that clearly defines the core problem.

### 3. Problem Statement Structure (Single Paragraph vs. Multiple RQs)
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** **Single continuous paragraph** is preferred for the main Problem Definition.
* **Details:** Present the core problem as a unified narrative. If you use Research Questions (RQs) elsewhere, ensure they seamlessly map back to this central problem statement.

### 4. Narrative Driven Approach (Technology-Driven vs. Problem-Driven)
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** Your work is naturally **Technology-Driven**, which is completely acceptable.
* **Details:** In practice, reviewers do not strictly penalize choosing one narrative over the other. Since you selected specific techniques (Query2Doc, CSQE, hybrid fusion) to solve the underlying Arabic IR bottleneck, framing the narrative around evaluating and adapting these technologies to solve the problem is correct.

### 5. Presenting Exploratory Objectives
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** Present them as **clear, explicit, and measurable goals** aligned directly with your final pipeline.
* **Details:** The evaluation committee evaluates the project based on a strict 1-to-1 mapping:
  $$\text{Problem Definition} \longleftrightarrow \text{Objectives} \longleftrightarrow \text{Methodology \& Results} \longleftrightarrow \text{Conclusions}$$
  Even if your process was exploratory, your written objectives must cleanly state what was tested, evaluated, and achieved so that each objective can be checked off in the conclusion.

### 6. Thesis Layout Section Formatting
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** Must be written as **one single continuous paragraph** (No bullet points).
* **Details:** The section at the end of Chapter 1 (Thesis Layout / Outline) should be one long paragraph summarizing the structure of the manuscript (e.g., *"Chapter 2 establishes... Chapter 3 details... Chapter 4 reports..."*). This provides the reader with a smooth structural overview.

### 7. Arabic Language Processing Challenges Formatting
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** Maintain **clear sub-headings** with explanatory paragraphs.
* **Details:** Sub-headings (e.g., Morphological Richness, Diglossia, Orthographic Variations) followed by focused text allow the reader to easily digest distinct linguistic bottlenecks.

### 8. Expected Abstract Length
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** **$\approx \frac{3}{4}$ of a page** (roughly 250–350 words).
* **Details:** 
  * It **must fit on a single page** (should not spill over onto a second page).
  * It should not be shorter than half a page.
  * Formatted using standard thesis spacing: **Times New Roman, Size 12, 1.5 line spacing**.
  * Must summarize: *Context/Area $\rightarrow$ Problem $\rightarrow$ Objectives $\rightarrow$ Methodology $\rightarrow$ Key Findings $\rightarrow$ Overall Conclusion*.

### 9. Arabic Abstract (المستخلص) & Technical Terminology
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** **Required**. Full Arabization of technical terms is strongly preferred.
* **Details:**
  * **Translation/Arabization:** You should translate/arabize technical terms into formal Arabic wherever standard Arabic equivalents exist (e.g., *الاسترجاع الكثيف* for Dense Retrieval, *التوليد المعزز بالاسترجاع* for RAG).
  * **English Terms in Parentheses:** If an acronym or term lacks a natural Arabic equivalent, write the Arabic translation/transliteration first, followed by the English acronym in parentheses upon first mention.
  * **Numerals:** Use standard ASCII/Western Arabic numerals (**0, 1, 2, 3...**) consistently across both English and Arabic sections to maintain uniform formatting.

### 10. Tables vs. Figures Preference & Page Limits
* **Dr. Tahani’s Guidance:** **Do NOT confuse Tables and Figures**. Keep the main body under **100 pages**.
* **Rules on Tables vs. Figures:**
  * Tables and Figures are distinct entity types and must never be merged or mislabeled.
  * **Table Captions:** Must always be placed **ABOVE / TOP** of the table.
  * **Figure Captions:** Must always be placed **BELOW / BOTTOM** of the figure.
  * Each must have its own numbering sequence per chapter (e.g., *Table 3.1* vs. *Figure 3.1*).
* **Page Limit & Appendix Strategy:**
  * The core manuscript (Chapters 1 to 5) should ideally be concise (**"ما قلّ ودل"**) and not exceed **100 pages**.
  * Large code snippets, detailed mathematical proofs, or raw extra data tables **must be moved to Appendices** (e.g., *Appendix A*, *Appendix B*). Appendix pages do **not** count toward the primary thesis page limit.

### B. List of Abbreviations Formatting
* Must be sorted **Alphabetically (A to Z)**.
* **First Mention Rule:** When any abbreviation appears for the first time in the main thesis text, write the **full phrase first**, followed by the abbreviation in parentheses. Example: 
  > *"Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems..."*
* Subsequent mentions should use the abbreviation alone (e.g., *"RAG"*).

### C. Citation and Reference Formatting
* **Sequential Ordering:** Citations must strictly follow the IEEE order-of-appearance format (`[1]`, `[2]`, `[3]...`) as they appear throughout the text.
* **Web References:** Any online link or URL cited in the references must include:
  * Full URL link.
  * Explicit access date (e.g., *"[Online]. Available: https://... [Accessed: March 2026]"*).
