\chapter*{Abstract}

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems depend on effective information retrieval to ground Large Language Model (LLM) outputs in external knowledge, yet Arabic information retrieval faces severe challenges stemming from morphological richness, orthographic variation, and vocabulary mismatch between queries and documents. Query enhancement (QE)---the modification of a user's query before retrieval---offers a modular, retriever-agnostic intervention to address these challenges. However, existing QE techniques were developed for English using proprietary models with 175 billion or more parameters, and their transferability to Arabic using small, open-source models has not been systematically investigated.

This thesis investigates the extent to which small open-source LLMs can improve Arabic information retrieval through QE, and identifies the model characteristics that determine effectiveness. Experiments were conducted on the Multilingual Information Retrieval Across a Continuum of Languages (MIRACL) Arabic benchmark, comprising 2,896 queries and 2.06 million passages, using the Multilingual Dense Passage Retriever (mDPR) for dense retrieval and BM25S, a Python implementation of Best Matching 25 (BM25), for sparse retrieval. Error analysis on the baseline identified a 34\% query failure rate and the pronounced underperformance of very short queries, motivating the adaptation of the Query2Doc technique for Arabic zero-shot application. Ten open-source LLMs with 2--8 billion parameters were evaluated using a standardised protocol, with all experiments executed on Google Colab graphics processing units (GPUs).

QE improved dense retrieval for all nine viable models, with Normalised Discounted Cumulative Gain at rank 10 (NDCG@10) improvements ranging from +3.7\% to +23.5\%. Aya Expanse 8B achieved the highest overall improvement (+23.5\% dense, +9.2\% BM25), while Jais-2 8B was the most effective model for sparse retrieval (+10.8\%). Zero-shot Arabic QE with a 3-billion-parameter model exceeded the original Query2Doc paper's few-shot English results obtained with a 175-billion-parameter model. However, BM25 retrieval was degraded by six of nine models due to term dilution, demonstrating that QE strategies must be adapted to the retrieval paradigm. Model size was positively correlated with dense retrieval improvement, and training data diversity was found to be a more significant predictor of query expansion quality than Arabic-specific benchmark scores.

The proposed corpus-steered pipeline, combined with hybrid BM25+Dense fusion, achieved 0.7137 NDCG@10 on the MIRACL Arabic benchmark---a 54.5\% improvement over the BM25 baseline and a 13.9\% improvement over a strong hybrid system without QE.
